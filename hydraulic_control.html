A pump, a four-way servo valve, two metres of hose and a differential cylinder pressing a specimen. A digital controller drives the ram to the specimen in position control, then switches to force control, holds the force, releases, and retracts. Loop analysis

The loop gain is L(z) = K(z)·z⁻¹·G(z): G is the zero-order-hold discretization of the plant linearized at the operating point, z⁻¹ the one-sample computation delay, K the discrete controller. Stability is decided by the closed-loop eigenvalues (all |z| < 1); the margins and the Nyquist curve say how close it is.

The model, equation by equation

1 · What you are looking at

Pick up a garden hose full of water and swing one end: the water lags, then pushes back. Two metres of oil in a 12 mm hose weigh about 0.2 kg, but squeezed through that narrow bore they behave like a heavy slug. Inertance I = ρLh/Ah ≈ 1.5·10⁷ kg/m⁴ sits between two springs made of oil: the small volume at the valve port and the cap chamber of the cylinder. Oil is stiff (β ≈ 1.5 GPa) only when it is free of air. Half a percent of entrained air at atmospheric pressure turns it into something closer to a stiff rubber: βeff drops to about 28 MPa at zero gauge pressure. The ram, 160 kg, rides on these springs. The controller sees all of it through a position sensor, a load cell and a 1 ms clock.

2 · The servo valve

The spool is a second-order system driven by the command u ∈ [−1, 1]. Each metering edge is a sharp-edged orifice, so the flow grows with the square root of the pressure drop; a small regularization (ptr = 0.1 MPa) removes the infinite slope at zero drop. The valve is critically lapped: at xv = 0 only the internal leakage KL flows.

ẍ_v + 2ζ_v·ω_v·ẋ_v + ω_v²·x_v = ω_v²·u ω_v = 2π f_v, ζ_v = 0.7 x̂ = clamp(x_v, −1, 1), x⁺ = max(x̂, 0), x⁻ = min(x̂, 0), g(Δp) = Δp / (Δp² + p_tr²)^¼ ≈ sign(Δp)·√|Δp| Q_A = K_v·[x⁺·g(p_s − p_a) + x⁻·g(p_a − p_T)] + K_L·(p_s − 2p_a + p_T) Q_B = −K_v·[x⁺·g(p_B − p_T) + x⁻·g(p_s − p_B)] + K_L·(p_s − 2p_B + p_T) Q_S = K_v·[x⁺·g(p_s − p_a) − x⁻·g(p_s − p_B)] + K_L·(2p_s − p_a − p_B) K_v = Q_N / √(Δp_N/2), Q_N = 100 L/min at Δp_N = 7 MPa, K_L = 2·10⁻¹³ m³/(s·Pa)

Notice what this does to a linear analysis: opening the valve towards the cap side meters against ps − pa, closing it meters against pa. The flow gain on the two sides of xv = 0 differs, and the model is not differentiable there.

3 · Oil that is not quite incompressible

Entrained air of volume fraction α₀ at atmospheric pressure p₀ is squeezed polytropically (n = 1.4). Mixing the compliances of oil and air gives

α(p) = α₀/(1 − α₀)·(p₀/p_abs)^(1/n), β_eff(p) = β_oil·(1 + α) / (1 + α·β_oil/(n·p_abs)), p_abs = p + p₀

At 10 MPa the air is crushed and βeff ≈ 1.47 GPa; near zero gauge pressure it collapses to tens of MPa, and towards vacuum it goes to zero, which also keeps the pressure from falling below absolute zero.

4 · Volumes, the hose, and the ram

ṗ_a = β(p_a)/V_a·(Q_A − Q_h) V_a = V_a0 + V_h/2 I·Q̇_h = p_a − p_A − R_lam·Q_h − K_t·Q_h·|Q_h| I = ρL_h/A_h, R_lam = 128·μ·L_h/(π·d_h⁴), K_t = ρ(f·L_h/d_h + K_m)/(2A_h²) ṗ_A = β(p_A)/(V_A0 + V_h/2 + A_A·y)·(Q_h − A_A·ẏ) ṗ_B = β(p_B)/(V_B0 + A_B·(S − y))·(Q_B + A_B·ẏ) m·ÿ = p_A·A_A − p_B·A_B − m·g − F_f(ẏ) − F_w(y, ẏ) + F_stop(y, ẏ) F_f = F_c·tanh(ẏ/v_s)·[1 + (k_st − 1)·exp(−(ẏ/v_str)²)] + b_v·ẏ (Stribeck: break-away 1.3·F_c) F_w = max(0, k_w·δ + c_w·δ̇), δ = y − y_c > 0 (the specimen can push, not pull) F_stop = max(0, −k_s·y − c_s·ẏ), y < 0 (bottom stop)

Bore 120 mm, rod 80 mm: AA = 113.1 cm², AB = 62.8 cm². Stroke S = 200 mm, the specimen starts at yc = 120 mm. Ram plus tooling m = 10 kg + M.

5 · The digital controller

Every Ts the controller samples y and F, computes u, and the valve receives it one sample later (computation delay); between samples u is held constant (zero-order hold). A supervisor decides which law runs:

  • settle (0.1 s): hold y_ref = 0. approach: quintic profile to yc − 2 mm in 1 s. creep: y_ref rises at 10 mm/s until the load cell reads more than 1 kN.
  • press: force control, F_ref ramps to its target in 0.25 s and holds 0.5 s. release: ramps down to 1 kN in 0.25 s.
  • retract: position control, quintic back to y = 0 in 1.8 s. done: hold 0.3 s.
position: e_k = y_ref − y_k D_k = T_f/(T_f+T_s)·D_(k−1) + K_d/(T_f+T_s)·(e_k − e_(k−1)) filtered derivative, T_f = 2 ms I_k = I_(k−1) + K_i·T_s·e_k (frozen while saturated in the same direction: anti-windup) u_k = sat(K_p·e_k + I_k + D_k + K_ff·v_ref), K_ff = A_A / (K_v·√(p_s/2)) force: u_k = sat(K_pF·(F_ref − F_k) + I_F,k), I_F,k = I_F,(k−1) + K_iF·T_s·(F_ref − F_k) switching: the integrator of the new law is loaded with the last command, so u does not jump (bumpless transfer)

6 · Linearize, discretize, close the loop

At an operating point (x₀, u₀) where all states are steady except y, which drifts at the cruising speed, the eight plant states obey δẋ = A·δx + B·δu, with A and B taken by central differences of the nonlinear right-hand side. The zero-order hold turns this into an exact discrete model:

exp( [A B; 0 0]·T_s ) = [Φ Γ; 0 1] Φ = e^(A·T_s), Γ = ∫₀^T_s e^(A·σ) dσ · B G(z) = C·(zI − Φ)⁻¹·Γ K_pos(z) = K_p + K_i·T_s·z/(z−1) + K_d·(z−1)/((T_f+T_s)·z − T_f) L(z) = K(z)·z⁻¹·G(z), z = e^(jωT_s) closed loop: x⁺ = Φx + Γw, s⁺ = A_c s − B_c C x, w⁺ = C_c s − D_c C x

The closed-loop state contains the plant (8), the controller memory (3 for PID, 1 for PI) and the delayed command (1). The system is stable when every eigenvalue lies inside the unit circle. Each z maps back to a continuous pole s = ln(z)/Ts, which gives a frequency and a damping ratio you can feel.

7 · Where the energy goes

The pump delivers ps·QS. The valve burns psQS − paQA − pBQB as heat. The rest compresses oil (Σ p·V/β·ṗ), accelerates the hose slug (½IQh²) and the ram (½mẏ²), lifts the ram (mgy), heats the hose and the seals, and does work on the specimen and the stop. The page integrates every term separately; their sum must equal the pump work, and the check is shown under the scene.

8 · Problems

  1. Derive the hydraulic natural frequency of the ram with both chambers closed, ωh² = (βAAA²/VA + βBAB²/VB)/m. Evaluate it at y = 10 mm and y = 190 mm. Where in the stroke is the press softest, and why does a differential cylinder make this asymmetric?
  2. Treat the hose as a lumped mass between two compliances, Ca = Va/β and CA = VA/β. Show that the slosh mode has ω² = (1/Ca + 1/CA)/I and find it in the open-loop pole table. What happens to it when you double the hose length? When you halve the bore?
  3. Linearize the valve flow QA(xv, pa) at xv = 0⁺ and at xv = 0⁻. Show that the flow gain differs by √((ps − pa)/pa), and argue why a single linear model cannot describe a critically lapped valve at null.
  4. At standstill the smoothed Coulomb friction has slope Fc·kst/vs ≈ 10⁶ N·s/m. Explain why linearizing about ẏ = 0 then predicts an absurdly well damped ram, and what this tells you about using small-signal analysis on stick-slip systems.
  5. Set α₀ = 2 % and rerun. Explain from βeff(p) why the force loop barely changes while the start of the approach does.
  6. Increase Ts from 1 ms to 4 ms with the gains unchanged. Read off the phase margin before and after, and explain the loss using the phase of z⁻¹ and of the zero-order hold, −ωTs − ωTs/2.
  7. In force control the plant from spool to force is nearly an integrator. Show that for a stiff specimen dF/dt ≈ keff·Kq·xv/AA, with 1/keff = 1/kw + VA/(β·AA²). Use this to choose KpF for a 10 Hz crossover, then check your answer in the Bode plot.
  8. The bumpless transfer loads the integrator with the last command. Remove it on paper: what jump in u, and in force, would the switch from creep to press produce at the default gains?
  9. Find the largest Kp for which the position loop is stable. Compare it with the textbook rule Kv < 2ζhωh, where Kv is the velocity gain of the loop, and explain the difference.
